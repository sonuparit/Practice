# **Hiring Manager's Assessment: DevOps Engineer – 1 | Questhiring**

Perspective: Senior DevOps Engineer + Hiring Manager + Recruiter

I have reviewed both uploaded documents:

- `jd.txt` – actual job description.
- `company-research.md` – company intelligence report.

I will treat the JD as the primary source and use company research only for business context.

## **1. What am I actually looking for?**

If I were the Senior DevOps Engineer hiring for this position, I would evaluate six areas.

| Area                | What I expect from you                                                |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cloud               | Practical experience with AWS or GCP                                  |
| DevOps fundamentals | Understanding of CI/CD, infrastructure and deployment                 |
| Automation          | Ability to automate repetitive engineering tasks                      |
| Troubleshooting     | Ability to identify problems and find solutions                       |
| Communication       | Ability to explain technical problems clearly in writing and verbally |
| Collaboration       | Ability to work with developers and other engineering teams           |

**What matters most:** The JD explicitly emphasizes hands-on DevOps experience, cloud knowledge and communication skills.

It does not specify Kubernetes, Terraform, Docker, Jenkins or any particular monitoring tool as mandatory requirements.

I would therefore assess your fundamentals and practical experience rather than reject someone simply for not knowing a particular tool.

## **2. What problems is this role expected to solve?**

Based strictly on the JD, I expect you to help with:

1. **Cloud infrastructure:** Work with AWS or GCP infrastructure.
2. **Deployment processes:** Help deploy applications reliably.
3. **CI/CD:** Build, maintain or improve software delivery pipelines.
4. **Automation:** Reduce repetitive manual engineering work.
5. **Problem-solving:** Investigate and resolve infrastructure or deployment issues.
6. **Team collaboration:** Coordinate with engineers and communicate technical requirements.

The JD does not identify specific infrastructure failures, existing bottlenecks or ongoing engineering projects.

Therefore, these are responsibilities implied by the requirements, not confirmed problems within the organization.

## **3. Why does this role exist today?**

There are three important signals.

**First: Multiple openings.**

The company is recruiting:

- DevOps Engineer – 1: 2–4 years
- DevOps Engineer – 2: 4–6 years
- Senior DevOps Engineer: 6–8 years

This suggests a requirement for multiple experience levels.

**Second: Immediate joining.**

The JD explicitly says immediate joiners only. This indicates an urgent hiring requirement, although the reason for urgency is not disclosed.

**Third: Growing engineering requirements.**

Your company research identifies Questhiring's involvement in technology recruitment and staffing.

However, the vacancy describes these positions as joining its growing engineering team.

**My interpretation:** The company is looking to strengthen its DevOps engineering capacity, potentially for an internal team or a client engagement. The actual arrangement needs confirmation.

## **4. What kind of candidate would feel like a SAFE choice?**

If I were hiring, my ideal candidate would look like this:

**The safe candidate**

1. Has practical experience working with AWS or GCP.
2. Understands how CI/CD pipelines work end to end.
3. Can explain application deployment processes.
4. Can troubleshoot Linux, networking and infrastructure issues.
5. Understands automation and scripting.
6. Communicates technical problems clearly.
7. Takes ownership instead of waiting for someone else to solve every problem.
8. Can collaborate effectively with developers and senior engineers.
9. Is available to join immediately.

One additional point: I would prefer someone who honestly explains what they have implemented over someone who claims expertise in 30 technologies but cannot explain a single real implementation.

## **5. What kind of candidate would feel RISKY?**

| Candidate behaviour                         | Why I would hesitate                       |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Only theoretical knowledge                  | May struggle with actual infrastructure    |
| Cannot explain a CI/CD pipeline             | Weak understanding of delivery workflows   |
| Knows tools but cannot troubleshoot         | May require excessive supervision          |
| Poor communication                          | Difficult to collaborate during incidents  |
| Cannot explain previous projects            | Experience claims become questionable      |
| Blames others for failures                  | Lack of ownership                          |
| Exaggerates production experience           | Trust and reliability concerns             |
| Requires extensive guidance for basic tasks | May not meet the expected experience level |

**An important observation:** Communication is explicitly emphasized in this JD, even more clearly than any individual DevOps tool.

I would take that seriously.

## **6. If I had 30 seconds to decide whether to interview you, what would I expect on your resume?**

I would look for the following evidence in this order.

## **The 30-second resume checklist**

What I want to see immediately

1. **AWS hands-on experience**

   Evidence of working with EC2, VPC, IAM, EKS, S3 and other cloud services.
2. **Infrastructure as Code**

   Terraform modules, remote state management and automated provisioning.
3. **CI/CD implementation**

   GitHub Actions or Jenkins pipelines with actual build and deployment workflows.
4. **Containerization and deployment**

   Docker, Kubernetes, Helm and application deployment experience.
5. **Automation and troubleshooting**

   Python/Bash scripts, Linux administration and examples of resolving technical problems.
6. **Communication and ownership**

   Clear project descriptions demonstrating what you personally designed, implemented and resolved.

## **7. My final hiring decision**

Would consider interviewing

If your resume demonstrates the projects and implementations you have described, I would consider you for the technical interview.

But I would investigate three things during the interview:

1. Can you independently troubleshoot infrastructure and deployment failures?
2. Can you explain your architecture and technical decisions without relying on memorized answers?
3. Can you communicate effectively with developers and senior engineers?

### **What I want you to change in your presentation**

Don't present yourself as:

"I'm familiar with AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform and Jenkins."

Present yourself as:

"I'm capable of provisioning cloud infrastructure, deploying containerized applications, automating delivery workflows and implementing monitoring. I can explain the architecture, troubleshoot failures and improve operational processes."

That is a much stronger hiring proposition.

**My single most important advice:** Your technical skills may get you shortlisted, but your ability to explain your implementations, demonstrate problem-solving and communicate clearly will help you get selected.
